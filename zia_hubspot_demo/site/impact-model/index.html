Assumptions, and where this model is weakest

A model presented without its limits is a sales pitch. These are the places this one is soft, stated so they can be argued with rather than discovered later.

  • 4.33 weeks per month is used throughout (52 ÷ 12). Fine for planning, wrong for invoicing — real billing follows actual hours logged, which will differ.
  • Churn is modeled as run-rate loss, not as remaining contract value. A placement ending in month three costs more than one ending in month twenty, and this model doesn't distinguish them. It therefore understates early churn and overstates late churn.
  • The save rate is the softest input on the page. Health scoring surfaces at-risk placements; it does not save them. Whether an intervention works depends on the CSM and the client, not the CRM. Treat 25% as a hypothesis to be measured after two quarters, not a promise.
  • Coordination hours are self-reported and people are poor at estimating their own admin time — usually underestimating it. Worth timing one real onboarding before trusting the figure.
  • Talent cost as a flat percentage smooths over real variation between tiers and regions. Actual margin comes from a cost rate on each talent record, which is exactly why the architecture puts it there.
  • Nothing here counts the upside from faster matching, higher showcase conversion, or tier upgrades caught by scope-drift monitoring. Those are real but harder to defend, so they're left out rather than used to inflate the number.

The honest summary: the revenue-visibility figures are solid — they're arithmetic on rates and hours. The recovery figures are directional, dependent on an intervention rate nobody can know in advance. Present them as two different kinds of claim, because they are.

The size of the number matters less than the fact that, today, it can't be produced at all. A business whose revenue comes almost entirely from recurring delivery is currently forecasting on the one part of the transaction that only happens once. Everything in this model becomes reportable the moment a placement has a record of its own.

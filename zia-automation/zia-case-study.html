01 · The situation

What ZIA does, and why its CRM is hard

ZIA is an organizational development and talent solutions company. It sells leadership programs, org design and change management to growing businesses, and delivers them through consultants.

That makes it a two-sided business, and it is the reason the data model is not a standard sales pipeline. Revenue requires both sides to line up at once:

SideWhoIn the CRM
DemandClient organizations buying programs226 companies · 166 buyers
SupplyConsultants who deliver the work400 consultants

Sell without an available consultant and you cannot deliver. Hire without demand and you are paying people to sit idle. A product company only has to watch one funnel; ZIA has to watch two and the join between them. That join is what the CRM exists to make visible, and it is where every finding in this build came from.

02 · The audit

What I found before writing anything

The instinct is to start importing. I queried the live portal first, and the data contradicted the handover notes on every material point.

The import had run twice

Create timestamps clustered exactly: 10,000 records at 12:33 and 10,000 more at 12:36. The source file was 10,000 rows. Nobody had noticed.

Every deal association would have failed silently

The deal file matched companies on domain. That property was empty on all 20,000 records — an earlier "fix" had been applied to the deal files only. A live search for a sample domain returned zero companies. Importing as planned would have associated nothing and auto-created ~994 more companies on top.

The planned import route could not have worked at all

The deal file had no Pipeline or Deal Stage column. HubSpot requires both to create a deal, so the UI importer would have rejected the file regardless of how the columns were mapped.

Three more problems sat underneath:

Duplicate names10,000 rows carried only 1,525 unique names — one appeared 54 times
Dirty categories308 free-text sector values: dental, Dental, DSO, ortho, Ortho as separate rows
Ghost records535 nameless companies HubSpot auto-created from contact email domains
03 · What I built

Four layers, all through the API

I archived 20,550 records and rebuilt from a deduplicated source with domains populated, then layered the operating model on top.

The automation

The portal is free-tier, so workflows return 403 — the feature does not exist at this level. Rather than drop the requirement I moved orchestration outside the platform: eleven rules as scheduled API jobs, in version control, with an append-only run log.

Every rule reconciles current state instead of reacting to an event, so a missed run self-heals on the next pass and re-running is always safe.

The bug that justifies the whole approach

A dry run reported alreadyCovered 500 yet wouldWrite 817 — arithmetic that cannot be right. HubSpot returns association toObjectId as a number while object ids everywhere else are strings, so every deduplication check silently missed. Live, that was 817 duplicate tickets. Fixed once in the client layer so no rule can reintroduce it. The second live run wrote zero — which is the property that makes any of this safe to schedule.

The billing boundary

HubSpot Payments needs a US entity and a connected bank, and a consulting firm billing on contract terms should not route payments through its CRM anyway. So billing is modelled as an integration boundary: the accounting system owns the ledger, and seven deal properties carry the invoice state back so revenue operations can report on collections without leaving HubSpot. Those are exactly the fields a QuickBooks Online or Xero connector writes.

04 · What it revealed

Three things the business could not see

None of these are CRM problems. They are business problems the CRM was hiding, and they only became askable once the data was trustworthy.

The premium tier is the hardest to sell and delivers no better

12.2% vs 33.9%

Summit closes 9 of 82 pursuits — a 12.2% win rate against Core's 33.9% — and carries the highest price at $51,395 a sale. Yet delivered health is flat across all three tiers: 68.3 / 68.3 / 70.3. The company works hardest to sell the thing clients experience no differently. Nobody had put conversion and delivered outcome in the same view, so the question could not be asked.

A fifth of the bench cannot be placed at all

29 / 187

21 lapsed credentials and 8 never started, with a further 17 expiring. These consultants cannot be assigned — capacity frozen by an administrative process nobody owned. Worse, the bench field said otherwise: 153 consultants were on live engagements while only 76 were marked as placed, so half the delivery team looked bookable while already working. That is how you promise a client someone who has no hours left. WF-12 now derives bench state from actual assignment, and compliance always wins.

The risk flag understates exposure seven times over

58 vs 8

58 engagements score below 60 against a target of 70, but only 8 carry At Risk status. The score is recomputed from live delivery signals on every run; the status field is not. Nothing writes the model's verdict back to the flag the business actually filters and reports on, so the CRM understates its own exposure by seven times. Catching drift at week three instead of month three is the difference between a save and a refund.

Receivables have stopped moving, not slowed down

40 of 44

40 of 44 unpaid invoices are more than 90 days past due, against an average time-to-pay of 36.2 days. A long tail behind a short average is not slow payment — it is an absent process. $967,510 has already been written off, which is what the rest becomes if nobody chases. WF-07 now raises a ticket per past-due invoice with priority scaled by age.

Nearly four sales in ten are discounted

39.0%

168 of 431 line items carry a discount, averaging 10.9% off and giving away $1,379,284 of list value. No one decides to discount at that rate; it accumulates one deal at a time, and without a line-item report nobody sees the total.

One architectural note worth saying out loud: delivery engagements are also Closed Won. Counting them as sales gives a 55.9% win rate. Excluding them gives the real 30.1%. 07 · Application questions

Drafted answers

Grounded in this build. The two marked YOURS require your real history — never let anyone else write those.

Years of hands-on HubSpot experience? YOURS

Answer honestly. If this build is the bulk of your hands-on time, say so and pivot: "Most of my depth is API and architecture rather than years in the UI — here is a portal I designed and populated end to end." A verifiable artifact beats an inflated number, and the number is checkable.

Which Hubs have you worked in?

Sales Hub (pipelines, deals, quotes, line items, products), Service Hub (ticket pipelines and service automation), and the CRM platform itself (custom properties, associations, lifecycle). Marketing Hub is the gap — say so directly and note the JD lists it as something you would own, not something you must arrive with.

Have you built automated workflows?

Yes — eleven, covering lead routing, lifecycle sync, onboarding, health monitoring, SLA escalation, compliance, collections, inbound lead processing, lead scoring with territory routing, an NPS feedback loop and a five-signal engagement health model. Because the portal was free-tier, I built them as scheduled API jobs rather than native workflows. Each is idempotent: it reconciles state rather than reacting to an event, so a missed run self-heals. I can port them to native workflows on a Professional portal without changing the logic.

Have you designed or restructured a CRM from the ground up?

Yes. I inherited 20,551 company records created by a duplicated import, with the domain field empty portal-wide, meaning no deal could associate to any company. I archived 20,550, rebuilt from a deduplicated source with domains populated, normalized 308 free-text category values into a controlled dropdown, and re-established every association. The portal now holds ~4,600 objects across eight types with no orphans.

What have you personally created in HubSpot?

56 custom properties across companies, contacts, deals and tickets; a two-sided data model separating client buyers from delivery consultants; a product library with line items and discounting; quotes priced from line items; a ticket taxonomy driving five service workflows; an invoice mirror modelling the accounting integration boundary; seven automation rules; and an operational dashboard. All built through the API and reproducible from scripts.

Describe a time you improved a business process. Problem, what you built, outcome.

Problem. A two-sided consulting business could not answer its most important question: how much of its consultant bench was earning. Contacts and deals had been loaded against different company sets, the company domain field was empty so nothing associated, and 40% of company records were empty shells.

What I built. Rebuilt the data model so both sides of the business — buyers and consultants — resolve to the same accounts. Added bench status, compliance status and coverage band as controlled properties, then built automation to keep them true: a compliance rule that holds lapsed consultants off the bench automatically, and a health rule that opens a review before an engagement fails.

Outcome. The bench became measurable for the first time. 18 of 187 consultants are bench-ready and idle — roughly $1.29M of unrealized annual margin — and a further 29 cannot be placed at all on compliance grounds, about $2.07M of frozen capacity. The sharper finding was that the bench field itself was wrong: 153 consultants were on live engagements while only 76 were marked placed, so half the delivery team looked bookable while already working. None of these numbers existed before, and all three are now maintained by automation rather than by hand.

How comfortable are you building reports and executive dashboards?

Comfortable, and opinionated about it. I built an operations dashboard covering pipeline, delivery health, bench readiness and collections, plus specifications for eighteen native HubSpot reports across executive, operations and sales views. The part that matters is not the chart — it is knowing that delivery engagements are also Closed Won, so any sales report that does not exclude them reports a 55.9% win rate instead of the real 30.1%.

08 · Caveats

What to disclose before you are asked

Volunteering these makes you credible. Being caught on them does the opposite.

ItemThe honest position
SyntheticThe data is generated. Deal stages, health scores, invoice states and all activity are modelled, not observed. The architecture and the method are the deliverable.
Free tierWorkflows, active lists and custom objects return 403. Native workflows and the custom report builder need Professional.
One pipelineFree tier allows one deal pipeline, so delivery engagements share the sales pipeline. On a paid portal they would be separated — which is the correct architecture and worth saying.
Contact cap353 of 1,000, after pruning back from 1,002 to reopen headroom. 14 of 218 engagements have no consultant linked because that person exceeded the cap.
Create DateImmutable on companies and contacts, so those show today. Mitigated with a Client Since property — which is what you would do on a real migration.
PaymentsDeliberately not connected. Needs a US entity, and consulting billed on contract terms belongs in the accounting system.

One rule for the interview: every number you cite should be one you can pull up live. If it is not on the dashboard or queryable in the portal, do not say it.

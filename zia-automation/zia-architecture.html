ZIA · HubSpot system design

How the ZIA Portal Is Wired

Four drawings of the parts that carry weight: where records come from and go, how one contact object holds two opposite kinds of person, why the automation reads before it writes, and where the CRM deliberately stops.

Figure 01

The whole system, and which side of the line each part sits on

HubSpot is the system of record. Everything to its right exists because the free tier will not run it — and every one of those arrows is a REST call, not an integration HubSpot knows about.

Figure 02

One object, two opposite populations

A talent business is two-sided: organizations that buy programmes, and consultants who deliver them. HubSpot gives you one Contact object and one Deal object. Custom objects are Enterprise-only, so both sides share the same objects and are separated by a discriminator property — which is what makes every report either correct or quietly wrong.

Figure 03

Why the automation reads before it writes

This is the load-bearing decision in the build. A HubSpot workflow is event-driven: a record crosses a threshold, the action fires once. Our engine is state-reconciling: it asks what is true now, compares it to what should be true, and writes only the difference.

The bug the dry run caught HubSpot returns association toObjectId as a number while object ids everywhere else are strings. Every dedupe Set.has(deal.id) silently missed. The dry run printed alreadyCovered: 500 and wouldWrite: 817 together — an impossible pair. Coerced once in lib/hubspot.js, so no rule can reintroduce it.

Figure 04

Where the CRM stops

HubSpot Payments needs a US entity and a bank connection; Stripe needs a merchant account. Neither exists — and a general ledger does not belong in a CRM regardless. So billing is modelled at the boundary: seven properties on the deal mirror invoice state back from wherever the ledger actually lives.
